Where did the Ledger / CryptoBilis money go?

An independent trace of the 9 October 2026 wallet drain (devices sold by the reseller CryptoBilis), rebuilt from public blockchain data across six chains.

Key findings

  • Most of the money is still visible. About 82% of the stolen value sits in wallets the attacker controls, mostly as ETH and the 203.79 BTC that have never moved. Another 10% is frozen.
  • TRON USDT left TRON within hours via THORChain, the USDT0 bridge, Relay, Chainflip, Bitget, HiFiSwap and NEAR Intents, and became ETH. Before that, $23M was cycled through USDD, which Tether cannot freeze.
  • Tornado Cash → Zcash → USDC round trip. About 95% of the 6,837 ETH deposited into Tornado was traced back out. Most of it was swapped into shielded Zcash, and about $11M then came back as USDC/ETH through NEAR Intents (timing and amount match).
  • "Custodial" swap services are traceable. Orders at HiFiSwap, Bitget, SWFT and Aeroswap match payouts 1:1 within seconds to minutes, at a constant exchange rate.
  • Cash-out has begun. Deposits went to HitBTC, ChangeHero and Binance, and about $2.6M went to OTC-broker-like wallets on TRON.
  • The earliest attacker action was at 03:55 UTC: a permission lock-out key on a TRON victim wallet, about an hour before the time previously reported.

Leads for freezing & attribution

  • Circle: 2.38M USDC in two attacker wallets that has not been frozen: 0x4600…2a6a and 0x3635…ee17.
  • Tether: 1.35M USDT that has not been frozen, at TMAXFF…BLicYy.
  • NEAR Intents: 800k USDT from two failed swaps was never refunded. Its refund address is the attacker's own wallet.
  • Binance: an established deposit address, in use since March 2026, received 27 ETH: 0x5D77…B211. This is the strongest identity lead.
  • Bybit: a withdrawal on 24 Sep funded the attacker's TRON control wallet (tx b284a254…).
  • HitBTC, ChangeHero, Bitget, SWFT, Aeroswap, HiFiSwap, Relay, Chainflip: their account and order records cover the deposits shown in the map. All exchanges should flag the BTC wallets and the Ethereum holding wallets.

If you are a victim: report to local police and to Ledger, and beware of "fund recovery" offers — they are a common follow-up scam.

Method & caveats

  • Data: public block explorers and node APIs only (mempool.space, TronGrid, Blockscout, public RPCs for Ethereum, BNB Chain, Polygon and Solana) plus the public status APIs of Relay, NEAR Intents and LI.FI. THORChain swaps were resolved from the memos written on-chain.
  • Confidence: transfers and balances are facts on-chain. Which wallets belong to the attacker is partly inferred from behaviour (fresh wallets that only relay attacker funds, matching timing/amounts) and marked as such in the address lists; addresses published by other researchers or linked by a service's own records are marked confirmed.
  • Mixers: Tornado Cash deposits cannot be mathematically linked to their withdrawals. The links shown are either confirmed, meaning the withdrawals merged into known attacker wallets or a service's records tie them together, or high-confidence heuristics. Those heuristics combine the pool-flow excess over a baseline week, withdrawal bursts to fresh wallets, shared gas sources and identical onward swaps to Zcash.
  • Instant-swap services without public order APIs (Bitget, SWFT, Aeroswap, HiFiSwap) were matched deposit-to-payout by timing and implied exchange rate. Every multi-order service matched 1:1.
  • Victims' privacy: victim wallet addresses are deliberately not published here; victims appear only as per-chain totals.
  • Values: USD amounts are estimates at the time of each transfer; balances "now" use current prices. Native BNB/POL movements are not covered (no keyless indexer), a gap of roughly $40k.
  • This is an independent analysis, not legal advice or an official statement by Ledger or any service mentioned. Corrections are welcome.
